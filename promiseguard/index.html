How it works

1. Your client approves the facts

Phone, prices, service area, booking URL, and any promises that must no longer appear, written down as simple rules.

2. We check the pages

Each page is compared with those approved rules: required text, forbidden text, and exact link destinations.

3. You get exceptions with evidence

A short report showing what failed and the exact text found, so your team can fix it before the client or a customer notices.

What it does and doesn't do (today)

Does

  • Checks page text against approved required and forbidden phrases
  • Checks that links point to the exact approved destination
  • Produces timestamped reports with evidence

Doesn't yet

  • Render JavaScript-heavy pages
  • Run fully self-serve on a schedule (pilots are founder-assisted)
  • Judge whether a claim is legally accurate; a REVIEW means "a person should look"

FAQ

Isn't this just change detection?

Change detection tells you a page changed. PromiseGuard checks a page against facts your client approved, so it can flag a wrong price that has been wrong for months, and stay quiet about harmless edits.

Do you need access to our clients' sites?

No logins. We check public pages you authorize. We only check sites you manage or have permission to review.

Will it catch everything?

No. It checks the rules you set up. A missing rule means a missing check, and formatting quirks can cause false alerts, which is why every result shows evidence for a person to review.

Is the code open?

Yes, the prototype checker is MIT-licensed on GitHub. The pilot adds setup, rule-writing and reporting done with you.
